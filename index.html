About

A modest bistro
with serious intent.

"We make British food with a classic or modern slant and source our ingredients from the local fields, woodlands and waters."

We use free range eggs, milk and cream from farms up the road. We serve a selection of local marsh lamb and pork, and fresh fish landed just a few hundred metres from us — the gurnard, whose tail graces our sign, was caught in Rye Bay itself.

You'll start your meal with our own sourdough bread. Late every evening we set a 'starter' to froth naturally; the morning after flour is added, it proves slowly all day and comes steaming hot from the oven the moment we open.

Every pudding, every sauce — made in-house. Real cream, butter, free range eggs, fresh fruit, real vanilla and quality chocolate. No mixes, no factory bases.

In Our Kitchen

Where it
all starts.

Stocks slowly simmering in pots, meats and fish being slow-cooked, sauces reducing in the pans, vegetables gently cooked to order. Our food is British with a classic or modern slant. Some bistro favourites stay on the menu all year; other dishes change with the seasons.

Our fish is what was genuinely caught in nets by fishermen off the south coast — Lemon Sole, Cod, Scallops, Squid and the odd Gurnard, depending on season and weather. The lamb has lived off the slightly salty grass of Romney Marsh. Our game — rabbit, venison, pigeon or mallard — has roamed the local woodlands.

Our forager brings his latest finds of mushrooms, wild herbs and berries all year round. Martin, our chef, grows herbs, quinces, berries and edible flowers on his allotment and in our back garden. Local producers provide spring water, ale, cider and a few local wines.

Every season brings its own worth-waiting-for: the wild blackberries and mushrooms of autumn, Jerusalem artichokes and purple sprouting broccoli in winter, asparagus and rhubarb in spring, and all the herbs, fruits and vegetables of the summer harvest.

Head Chef

Martin Peacock

"Having a restaurant and a chance to cook felt like a rebirth."

Martin has always liked cooking — even as a kid. Part greed, but also the challenge of getting it right, and other people's enjoyment. Before the restaurant he spent 27 years as a university lecturer. Cooking felt like something else entirely.

Having overcome the effects of a severe stroke, he still describes those evenings — candles lit, producing unpretentious food for appreciative customers — as feeling lucky.
